# How a screen gets designed

Scope: every product with a screen, in either stack. Open when: a screen is new or reworked, before any code for it.

His ruling, 2 October 2026: the one order for all visual front-end work. He steers the look and approves by looking; he never reads a spec. This page overrides `CHARTER.md` §5 and §7 on the visual workbench.

1. **Style.** The Juku style is the distillation rule and spatial layer below, and nothing else. He says how this product differs; adapt it a little, keep the core philosophy. Write the product's version in its `design/` folder.
2. **Brief.** The CTO briefs the GPT Space visualizer (`@Visualize`) on users, the real task, states, verified figures and fixed rules, with the distillation rule below. Never the layout. It seeks the most spectacular, beautiful, creative graphics within those limits. It never changes a rule or invents a number.
3. **Rounds.** He sends screenshots; the CTO answers the visualizer with guidance. At most three rounds. If looks are still open, he picks between the last two viable pictures. The CTO fixes any wrong figure or unusable screen before showing either.
4. **Save.** In `<product>-library/design/<screen>/`: the agreed screenshots with plain, findable titles, and a dated builder-only note (actions, states, rules, data, responsive changes, acceptance checks). He never reads the note. After release, add phone and desktop screenshots titled "shipped" with the date and pull request.
5. **Build.** A builder session builds straight from the saved screenshots and note into the real product, never a separate artefact. Claude Design is optional: use it only to settle an open layout, motion or responsive question, or to test a new interaction first. The builder checks displayed figures against independently worked examples and proves the real journey on phone and desktop, a preview, the reviewer. The preview is final. A needed departure from the agreed look goes to him first as before-and-after pictures.

**Distillation rule.** Distil complexity, never decorate it. Start from everything and reduce it to the smallest coherent mental model a first-time user needs. Do not trust the existing structure: design the information architecture itself. Decide what stays, merges, goes, appears after an action, or earns its own screen. Use the fewest layers that stay clear; every page, tab or step must earn its place. Simplify first, then disclose progressively; never hide clutter behind tabs. Infer what the system can work out; mark what was assumed and let the user correct it. Keep every business rule, permission and safety limit. For each screen ask: where am I, what matters, what next, understood in ten seconds? What does not help the user understand, decide or act goes or moves deeper.

**Spatial layer** (part of Juku style). Reference, inspected live, never copied: https://tarasovvitalii.com/demos/hypersolid/. Products feel like lightweight interactive 3D spaces: depth, cinematic movement, travelling through the interface, with direct touch: drag to move, pinch to zoom, tap to select or approach, inertia. Three.js/WebGL carries space, navigation and transitions; text, figures, forms and payments are always plain HTML controls. Essential journeys also work without 3D: keyboard and screen-reader paths, an alternative to every gesture, reduced motion. Plain controls load first; effects arrive progressively, light and compressed, with a graceful fallback on weak devices. Speed is a hard limit: usable within two seconds on a mid-range phone on normal mobile data, the plain interface before any 3D loads. One product across all devices.

**What stays.** Every screen obeys `design/SCREEN-LAW.md` and the product's constitution. A routine change to an existing screen goes straight in from the saved style and designs. A screenshot never proves the product works. The Architect session and screen spec are out of the default path; their files stay for now.
