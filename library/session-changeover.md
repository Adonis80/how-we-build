# Session changeover

Scope: every session, in either stack. Open when: the Juku OS Project's session starts, deciding to stay or start fresh, and before leaving one.

A session weighs staying against starting fresh at each boundary, decides that itself, and never starts fresh after barely any work (R-2026-09-10a, R-2026-09-25e, R-2026-09-26a; decision 0006, revised as [decision 0009](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/114)). Everything below works inside the limits of the first section.

## The limits

**One brief, and 300,000 tokens, held by machine** (R-2026-11-05a, R-2026-10-09b; [#363](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/363)). A session carries one brief, the work it was started with: the queue it names, top first, until a limit or its goal is met (*How much work*, below). At its checkpoint, record available usage and cost evidence, then stop or hand over when its brief is met or blocked. No repeated investigation without new evidence (#204, section 4). Reported plan usage is not cash and claims no saving until classified. At 300,000 tokens of context it hands over whatever the progress. A fresh session rebuilds its picture from the repository and the issues, never from the old chat; the handover note only points at them. This rule overrides amendment D of [decision 0016](../juku-library/DECISION-0016-LOCALISED-REVIEWS.md#D).

**The size hook** holds this. `.claude/settings.json` runs `.claude/size.py` before and after every tool call in a session started in a repository that carries it. It reads the newest main-thread turn's usage from the session's own transcript. Past 300,000 it demands the checkpoint after every call until one has gone through (a refused or failed call is not one). Past 350,000 it refuses every call but the checkpoint, loading a tool, and, once a checkpoint has gone through, starting the successor (*A session starts its own successor*). A checkpoint is a comment whose first line opens `Checkpoint:`, posted with the issue-comment tool, never the shell. At any size it refuses a checkpoint whose `Size:` line's `end` (or `now`) figure is not within 20k of the real one, and names the real one; `python3 .claude/size.py --now` prints it.

An unreadable transcript blocks nothing and the hook says so. `check.sh` runs the hook's selftest, which also holds the wiring. A session whose surface ran no hook (no `size hook: live` line at its first call) reads its own count and says so in its `Size:` line, source `unknown`. A session started on a surface that has not yet shown the hook says in its first checkpoint whether it ran.

**No earlier line** (R-2026-11-09a; [#363](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/363#issuecomment-6744266227)). No size below 300,000 is a reason to hand over, a wait included.

**Never idle on a read** (R-2026-11-05b). While one item waits on a read or a check and another ready item can be built, the session builds it meanwhile (`HOW-WE-BUILD.md`, *The loop*, owns this); when nothing else can be built, it waits as *After a wait* says.

**The shape of the cost.** The boot paid is sunk. Compare remaining work, verification and likely rework under continuation and a fresh start. No cache-age, clock or load threshold decides it, and no token or idle threshold but the ones above. Boots routinely dominating planned product slices mean wrong slicing: fix it where `next` lines are written.

## Starting work

**A product session starts with both repositories attached** ([#203](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/203), in #204 section 3): the product's and `Adonis80/how-we-build`. From the rulebook it may dispatch the existing asker (`ask-product-reads.yml`) and the recovery route (`review-product.yml`, `again` for a read that never finished: `library/product-joins.md`), and nothing else: a product session never changes rulebook files. The words that start one, and every handover to one, name both.

**Readiness before any deep read.** The first act on an item is to find, from its live body, the outcome asked and the next action this session can perform, verifying only the blockers deciding it: a secret, hands, his decision, an unmerged dependency. A `Ready:` line is a pointer, never an authority. A blocked action is parked with its unblock condition; the next eligible item is assessed. Missing information is unconfirmed readiness: settle it with a focused check; an investigation is its own bounded action.

## Staying or leaving

**At a boundary** (slice merged, parked with its blocker written, or advisory question settled) the session decides, never by reflex. It continues to the next eligible item, same repository and stack, only when all hold: the item is within its brief and the session is forecast to end it under 300,000 tokens (*How much work*); continuing is preferable to starting fresh; that item's next action is ready; retained understanding demonstrably helps: the session can state the item's bounded outcome, the evidence it will reuse and the reading still needed without reconstructing the last item; the rulebook permits it (below); no degradation or hard-stop condition is present. Before acting it verifies the target's head and diff, checks, reviews and dependencies, and a working tree clear of the item left, then records on the item taken `Continued: <from> → <to> — main <sha> — reuse <what>; next <outcome>`. Otherwise it checkpoints and leaves.

**Rules that land meanwhile.** Fix the governing revision at boot and each accepted boundary transition; take on no later rules mid-slice; resuming mid-slice after a gap, verify the branch, pull request and `main` facts first. At a boundary compare rulebook `main` with that revision. Reassess required pages, read those added or changed, then adopt the verified revision. A change to `HOW-WE-BUILD.md`, a required rebootstrap or inability to apply the changed rules ends the session.

**Stop on degradation** inside any item, on any one of: the repository disproves a material state the session asserted; the repeated-failure signs `HOW-WE-BUILD.md` owns under *When something goes wrong*; the session cannot state the slice, next action and open findings without reconstructing them; the surface reports the history compacted. Then no new work: finish the operation in hand, write and verify the handover, leave. A routine check failing, or an expected repository change, is neither boundary nor degradation: diagnose it inside the item.

**Leaving.** Every durable fact goes into GitHub; the pull request and roadmap handover stand alone. A product session's closing handover answers *Any material system improvement or independently sellable piece found?* (`library/ideas-for-later.md`). The checkpoint comment names its cause: `Checkpoint: boundary — slice complete | parked: <blocker> | advisory settled`, or `Checkpoint: degradation — <sign>`, or head office's release (*The Juku OS Project's session*), which carries no `Size:` line and counts as no record; the next session's first comment: `Resumed: clean`, or `Resumed: corrected — <fact>, wrong at handover | changed since`. Each `Checkpoint:`, `Resumed:` and `Continued:` line ends with its session's link where its surface gives one (a code-mode session's own address), so a reader can match it to the run that started it. A fact wrong at handover counts against continuing only with evidence linking it to carried assumptions. These lines are evidence for revising this page, not a measure.

## Starting the successor

**A session starts its own successor, at once, never on a clock** (R-2026-11-04e; [#314](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/314), item 2). Only once it has decided it must end, and checked that the repository carries what the next session needs, nothing pasted, it starts the successor if, and only if, an item's next action is ready for it (a parked permission act is ready only on its unblock condition: `library/his-opener.md`). When nothing can move it starts none (*After a wait*).

**The standing task.** It uses the one standing task of the repository it works in (a product session: the product's), created at first need with `create_trigger`, with no repeat and no run time, and later rewritten with `update_trigger`. The task's words name the repositories to attach, `HOW-WE-BUILD.md` read whole first, the checkpoint it starts from, its ticket where *The ticket* (below) gives it one, the queue, its `Planned:` line, and `Resumed:` first, unless a `Resumed:` or `Continued:` line is newer than that checkpoint, which means another session holds the repository and the successor does nothing more (*After a wait*). It fires the task with `fire_trigger`, then stops. A task with a repeat or a run time is a clock and never wakes a session.

**Where it lands.** A fired task starts a cloud session wherever the scheduler puts it; this page promises no surface. The baton route stands on any surface (R-2026-11-04f): its words carry all it needs. Starting a session with `create_session` is not a route.

**The clock lock** holds this by machine ([#378](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/378), from [#371](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/371); R-2026-11-05c). The size hook refuses a `create_trigger` or `update_trigger` carrying a `cron_expression` or `run_once_at`. It refuses a `fire_trigger` unless the newest the session saw of that task shows it with neither: a `create_trigger` that went through, an `update_trigger` whose result shows the task's own entry with neither set, or a `list_triggers` entry showing both empty. A field that entry leaves out is unset: the platform's result shows `"cron_expression": ""` and no `run_once_at`. An update's own words are never evidence, since one that omits the fields can leave an old schedule standing.

**Its connectors** (R-2026-11-09b; [Hemz-OS#285](https://github.com/Adonis80/Hemz-OS/issues/285#issuecomment-6744968242)). The task carries only the connectors its successor's job will call, since each one's tools load into every turn of that session, and one the job never calls is boot paid for nothing. One the job turns out to need is added at its next handover, named in that checkpoint as `Added:`.

**Its last line.** It then says "ready to start fresh session" naming the successor started. Starting none, or unable to, that line names *where* (a product's Project in Cowork; code mode, when needed, both repositories) and what to type. A successor that would wait on an approval is a permission, handled as `library/his-opener.md` says: parked, and taken by the next session he opens.

## Ticket and boot

**The ticket** (R-2026-11-05d; its shape is [#363's first comment](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/363#issuecomment-6656599619)). A successor on this repository, the Juku OS Project's session's firing included, or on a product: `HOW-WE-BUILD.md` is its start page and the README stays its index. Its words carry a `Ticket:` line and under it, one item a line, this paragraph first (`how-we-build:library/session-changeover.md#The ticket`), then the exact pieces its job needs beyond those two, each by address, its repository first (`how-we-build:library/reviewer.md#…`, `Hemz-OS:PRODUCT.md#5`), a whole file only where the job needs all of it, nothing in `juku-library/`, never loaded into a working session (`library/rulebook-files.md`). Its boot reads `HOW-WE-BUILD.md` whole, the README, the checkpoint its words name, the open pull requests, the item's live body (*Readiness before any deep read*), and each piece by `python3 product-reads/ticket.py --repo how-we-build=<its clone> read <address>` (a product's with `--repo <product>=<its clone>`; by hand without Python). No other library page, older checkpoint or whole issue thread at boot. Its first `Resumed:` or `Continued:` carries a `Boot:` line (*The boot measured*). At work, a read a live rule calls for (a page whose index row fires, a `Size:` search under *How much work*) is taken and goes in its next checkpoint as `Added: <address> — <why>`. Writing a ticket follows the rest of this section.

**A product's ticket** maps decision 0015's reads to addresses ([decision 0015](../juku-library/DECISION-0015-JUKU-OS-AUDIT.md), item 6): one product, named as the README's map spells it (`Hemz-OS:`), never a paused one; its `AGENTS.md` whole; `PRODUCT.md` by passage, never whole, its purpose and the rules it holds across every slice beside the job's own; `roadmap.json` by key, never whole, each of its notes it has (`_what_this_is`, `order_set`, `money_milestones`) and the item the job builds, its `retired` notes when an ideas review is due and a `done` item when its evidence is needed. Of the README's *Products under this rulebook* reads, the open items with their `next` lines are narrowed to the items its job names (R-2026-11-05d); the queue past them is read at work, as `Added:`. No product handover has carried a ticket yet.

**The check.** The task's words are the ticket's one home: each handover's `update_trigger` replaces them whole, never appending. Before firing, the outgoing session saves the words to a file and runs `python3 product-reads/ticket.py check --repo how-we-build=<its clone> <file>`, with `--repo <product>=<its clone>` for a product's. Every address must resolve to one piece; *The ticket* must be the first; the pieces total at most 40,000 tokens by its estimate (bytes ÷ 4). A product's ticket must name one live product, this repository's `library/carries-sessions.md` whole (which each product session reads at its start, that page says), the product's `AGENTS.md` whole, at least one passage each of `PRODUCT.md` and `roadmap.json` and none but a note over 2,000 tokens and half its file, each of those three notes the roadmap has, an item, and nothing in the product's `-library/`. Red, it splits the job, and the successor is never handed the excess.

Which passages serve the job, its purpose and cross-slice rules among them, is the outgoing session's choice, and nothing checks it: no reader sees a task's words, so a piece its successor finds missing goes in that successor's next checkpoint as `Added:` and the next ticket carries it.

**The guard** holds the ticket by machine, not by memory (R-2026-11-05c). The size hook (*One brief*) refuses a `create_trigger` or `update_trigger` whose words (the tools' `prompt`) carry a ticket failing that check, naming its faults, and a `fire_trigger` until such words have gone through in the same session.

**Where the guard acts.** Wherever the rulebook's `product-reads/ticket.py` is reachable: this repository, or a product whose session has this repository cloned beside it as `how-we-build`. It checks words against every clone it knows by the README map's name, so a product's ticket is refused where its product's clone is not beside it. A product's copy without the rulebook beside it does nothing of this, and its session runs the check by hand. No product carries the hook yet, so none has run there.

**At boot** the guard holds the boot reads. From the first call of a session whose task words (its first message before any reply that carries a `Ticket:` line) name a ticket, until its `Resumed:`, `Continued:` or `Checkpoint:` comment has gone through, it refuses a `Read` or `Grep` of a file in this repository, or in a product's clone beside it or the session's own, other than this repository's `HOW-WE-BUILD.md` and README or a whole-file piece. It also refuses a shell command naming a rule page (`library/`, `juku-library/`, `CHARTER.md`, `AGENTS.md`, `RICH-DATA.md`) or a product's `PRODUCT.md`, `roadmap.json` or `MAP.md` that is neither a whole-file piece nor, in a `ticket.py` command, a piece's file. It does not see a recursive search or a read dressed as a ticket read, and the older checkpoints and threads stay the session's to leave.

**The boot measured** (R-2026-11-05e; [#363](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/363#issuecomment-6665666753)). Wherever the guard acts, the size hook refuses a session's first `Resumed:` or `Continued:` comment unless it carries a `Boot:` line with the hook's own figures: `ours`, every tool result read before it (bytes ÷ 4, a loaded tool's schema excepted), and `platform`, the rest of its real size; past 30k ours, its three heaviest reads. The target for ours is under 30k. The refusal hands the line to paste, since a hook cannot post a comment itself. Not yet held by machine: the trim of those pieces goes in that session's next checkpoint as its next ticket's cut, and three boots over target in a row are flagged by hand on the queue's issue.

## Waiting and ending

**After a wait** (R-2026-11-04f; [#325](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/325) item 2). When nothing can move (a read, or a check it cannot watch, with no other ready item to build meanwhile: *Never idle on a read*, above), the session stays subscribed to the pull request's activity, writes its checkpoint, says so once on the pull request, and ends its turn: GitHub's event is the wake, never a clock. A surface that receives no such events, a Codex task among them, or one not yet shown to (so far only a code-mode cloud session has), counts on none. Woken, it first reads the open pull requests of the repository it works in: a `Resumed:` or `Continued:` line newer than its checkpoint means another session holds the repository (one builder at a time), and it does nothing more; an event that unblocks nothing ends the turn again, saying nothing. Otherwise it carries on, first posting `Resumed: clean` on the item, which takes ownership back, since its checkpoint gave it up (`library/two-stacks.md`). A wait on him wakes nothing: his reply restarts the work.

**The Juku OS Project's session** (head office; agreed under decision 0023, the debate on [#338](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/pull/338), from his question as the CTO recorded it on [#325](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/325#issuecomment-6629980663) item 5). He opens it himself and it sees none of the fired sessions' chats, so before anything else it reads the rulebook's chain: the standing task's last run (`list_triggers`: status, when fired, which session; not yet shown read from a Project session), the open pull requests, and the newest `Checkpoint:`, `Resumed:` and `Continued:` lines.

**The chain holds** the repository while that run is pending or running, or was fired after the newest checkpoint and has not failed; while a line is newer than that checkpoint (*After a wait*), unless the task's last run started the line's session (its link names it) and has failed; or while that checkpoint waits on a read or check not yet finished. Then head office builds and fires nothing here (one builder at a time).

**The chain has dropped** otherwise. An act parked for his consent (`library/his-opener.md`) head office takes itself first when his opener in its own session covers it; one the opener does not cover stays parked, put to him once as that page says, and holds up nothing else. While it holds such an act open (waiting on his edit, say) it fires nothing, building at most one other item itself as *The loop* allows. Then, holding none, with any other item ready it first releases a failed run's line by posting `Checkpoint: released — <the line's link>, its run failed before any checkpoint`, which makes the line older than a checkpoint for a successor's starter check (*A session starts its own successor*). It then rewrites the task's words for that work, naming the newest checkpoint as a successor's starter does, then fires it, never offering to (*Who decides*), since the chain builds without him; with none ready it fires nothing. The release was agreed under decision 0023, the debate on [#347](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/pull/347); none has yet run.

**Its first reply** tells him in one line what is running and what landed, and any act parked for his consent, with the opener to send if it covers it. Without the task tools, or reading no last run, it fires nothing, judges from the pull requests alone, and says so in that line.

**ARCHIVE** (R-2026-11-04f; #325 item 3). No tool archives or retitles a session, so a session's last message after its checkpoint, when no subscribed wait remains to wake it, ends with the word ARCHIVE, after its closing line: a label saying the session is finished, which he may archive from his list or leave. Nothing waits on it and no work rides on it, so it asks nothing of his hands.

**How much work** (R-2026-11-04g; agreed under decision 0023, the debate on [#326](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/pull/326)). Each item carries a class by its review rounds: S, one read; M, up to two rounds across pages; L, a gate, workflow or product slice with a journey. Every checkpoint records `Size: <session kind> boot <n>k end <n>k (<source>|unknown) items <#n class rounds, …>`, never a guess; with the size hook its source is `transcript`, and the hook refuses one off the real figure. At a boundary the next item is taken only if its class, by its kind's `Size:` records, is forecast to end under 300,000; with no records it is taken, and the line stops it as before. An item that does not fit hands over; none below it is taken first. The session that starts a successor writes `Planned: <kind> boot ~<n>k + <items by class> → end ~<n>k` (or `unknown`) in the task's words and its checkpoint. No page holds a weight: they live in those lines. The session that writes the fifth `Size:` line (found by searching the repository's comments for `Size:`) re-sets the classes from them in its successor's words or, if no session ended short with work ready, deletes this paragraph by pull request.

For Codex, the same continuation test applies across slices within a cloud task or thread.
