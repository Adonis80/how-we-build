# Session changeover

Scope: every session, in either stack. Open when: the Juku OS Project's session starts, deciding to stay or start fresh, and before leaving one.

His rulings: 10 September 2026, weigh staying against starting fresh at every turn; 25 September, the session decides that itself; 26 September, no fresh session after barely any work. Decision 0006, revised as [decision 0009](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/114).

**One brief, and 300,000 tokens, held by machine** (his rulings, 5 November 2026, 11:07, as filed on his word, "yes, file it", on [#363](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/363): *"Hard stop at 350,000 tokens, by machine. A hook reads the session's own transcript usage (the real figure, not an estimate) after every tool call: at 300,000 it requires the handover checkpoint; at 350,000 it blocks every action except writing that checkpoint. Every checkpoint carries a size line."*; this replaces his ruling of 9 October 2026 on [#174](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/174), *"hand over at about 400,000 tokens"*, whose brief clause stands: *"or when a brief's goal is met."*). A session carries one brief, the work it was started with: the queue it names, top first, until a limit or its goal is met (*How much work*, below; this widens #204's one deliverable to a queue, by the agreement cited there). At its checkpoint, record available usage and cost evidence, then stop or hand over when its brief is met or blocked. No repeated investigation without new evidence (agreed 13 October 2026, [#204](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/204#issuecomment-6145865695), section 4). Reported plan usage is not cash and claims no saving until classified. At 300,000 tokens of context it hands over whatever the progress. **The size hook** holds this: `.claude/settings.json` runs `.claude/size.py` before and after every tool call in a session started in a repository that carries it; it reads the newest main-thread turn's usage from the session's own transcript; past 300,000 it demands the checkpoint after every call until one has gone through (a refused or failed call is not one); past 350,000 it refuses every call but the checkpoint, loading a tool, and, once a checkpoint has gone through, starting the successor (*A session starts its own successor*); a checkpoint is a comment whose first line opens `Checkpoint:`, posted with the issue-comment tool, never the shell; at any size it refuses a checkpoint whose `Size:` line's `end` (or `now`) figure is not within 20k of the real one, and names the real one; `python3 .claude/size.py --now` prints it. An unreadable transcript blocks nothing and the hook says so. `check.sh` runs its selftest, which also holds the wiring. A session whose surface ran no hook (no `size hook: live` line at its first call) reads its own count and says so in its `Size:` line, source `unknown`. First shown on 5 November 2026, in the session that wrote it ([#366](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/pull/366)): once the files were on disk it ran on its calls and refused that session's own first checkpoint, whose `Size:` match a prose mention had caught (fixed, with its case). A session started on a surface that has not yet shown it says in its first checkpoint whether it ran. A fresh session rebuilds its picture from the repository and the issues, never from the old chat; the handover note only points at them. This overrides amendment D of [decision 0016](../juku-library/DECISION-0016-LOCALISED-REVIEWS.md#D) (*"No new session rule"*), which that frozen record keeps as history. **No earlier line** (his word, 9 November 2026, in the Juku OS Project, asked why a session at about 165,000 tokens had handed over before a wait: *"Where did you get the 150,000 token limit from? The limit is anything over 300,000."*, filed on [#363](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/363#issuecomment-6744266227)). No size below 300,000 is a reason to hand over, a wait included: this deletes his ruling of 12 October 2026 on [#174](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/174) (*"I agree with your second trigger. Make sure that this is applied globally."*), the hand-over before a quiet hour past about 150,000, which it supersedes on his later word. **Never idle on a read** (his word, 5 November 2026, on [#363](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/363#issuecomment-6665865830): *"if we load a session with lots of expensive context and then it sits idle for over an hour, the problem is that it's not doing any work in the meantime. So we want to avoid the situation where we have wasteful sessions like that … You are the CTO. Do your own research."*): while one item waits on a read or a check and another ready item can be built, the session builds it meanwhile (`HOW-WE-BUILD.md`, *The loop*: one waiting, one building, never more); when nothing else can be built, it waits as *After a wait* says. Everything below works inside these limits.

**The shape of the cost.** The boot paid is sunk. Compare remaining work, verification and likely rework under continuation and a fresh start. No cache-age, clock or load threshold decides it, and no token or idle threshold but the ones above. Boots routinely dominating planned product slices mean wrong slicing: fix it where `next` lines are written.

**A product session starts with both repositories attached** (agreed 13 October 2026, [#203](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/203), in #204 section 3): the product's and `Adonis80/how-we-build`. From the rulebook it may dispatch the existing asker (`ask-product-reads.yml`) and the recovery route (`review-product.yml`, `again` for a read that never finished: `library/product-joins.md`), and nothing else: a product session never changes rulebook files. The words that start one, and every handover to one, name both.

**Readiness before any deep read.** The first act on an item is to find, from its live body, the outcome asked and the next action this session can perform, verifying only the blockers deciding it: a secret, hands, his decision, an unmerged dependency. A `Ready:` line is a pointer, never an authority. A blocked action is parked with its unblock condition; the next eligible item is assessed. Missing information is unconfirmed readiness: settle it with a focused check; an investigation is its own bounded action.

**At a boundary** (slice merged, parked with its blocker written, or advisory question settled) the session decides, never by reflex. It continues to the next eligible item, same repository and stack, only when all hold: the item is within its brief and the session is forecast to end it under 300,000 tokens (*How much work*); continuing is preferable to starting fresh; that item's next action is ready; retained understanding demonstrably helps: the session can state the item's bounded outcome, the evidence it will reuse and the reading still needed without reconstructing the last item; the rulebook permits it (below); no degradation or hard-stop condition is present. Before acting it verifies the target's head and diff, checks, reviews and dependencies, and a working tree clear of the item left, then records on the item taken `Continued: <from> → <to> — main <sha> — reuse <what>; next <outcome>`. Otherwise it checkpoints and leaves.

**Rules that land meanwhile.** Fix the governing revision at boot and each accepted boundary transition; take on no later rules mid-slice; resuming mid-slice after a gap, verify the branch, pull request and `main` facts first. At a boundary compare rulebook `main` with that revision. Reassess required pages, read those added or changed, then adopt the verified revision. A change to `HOW-WE-BUILD.md`, a required rebootstrap or inability to apply the changed rules ends the session.

**Stop on degradation** inside any item, on any one of: the repository disproves a material state the session asserted; the repeated-failure signs `HOW-WE-BUILD.md` owns under *When something goes wrong*; the session cannot state the slice, next action and open findings without reconstructing them; the surface reports the history compacted. Then no new work: finish the operation in hand, write and verify the handover, leave. A routine check failing, or an expected repository change, is neither boundary nor degradation: diagnose it inside the item.

**Leaving.** Every durable fact goes into GitHub; the pull request and roadmap handover stand alone. A product session's closing handover answers *Any material system improvement or independently sellable piece found?* (`library/ideas-for-later.md`). The checkpoint comment names its cause: `Checkpoint: boundary — slice complete | parked: <blocker> | advisory settled`, or `Checkpoint: degradation — <sign>`, or head office's release (*The Juku OS Project's session*), which carries no `Size:` line and counts as no record; the next session's first comment: `Resumed: clean`, or `Resumed: corrected — <fact>, wrong at handover | changed since`. Each `Checkpoint:`, `Resumed:` and `Continued:` line ends with its session's link where its surface gives one (a code-mode session's own address), so a reader can match it to the run that started it. A fact wrong at handover counts against continuing only with evidence linking it to carried assumptions. These lines are evidence for revising this page, not a measure.

**A session starts its own successor, at once, never on a clock** (his ruling, 4 November 2026, 01:30, restated at 02:35: *"a session starts its own successor immediately, never on a clock; then do so at every handover"*; [#314](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/314), item 2). Only once it has decided it must end, and checked that the repository carries what the next session needs, nothing pasted, it starts the successor if, and only if, an item's next action is ready for it (a parked permission act is ready only on its unblock condition: `library/his-opener.md`): the one standing task of the repository it works in (a product session: the product's; created at first need with `create_trigger`, no repeat and no run time; later rewritten with `update_trigger`), its words naming the repositories to attach, `HOW-WE-BUILD.md` read whole first, the checkpoint it starts from, its ticket where *The ticket* (below) gives it one, the queue, its `Planned:` line, and `Resumed:` first unless a `Resumed:` or `Continued:` line is newer than that checkpoint, which means another session holds the repository and it does nothing more (*After a wait*), fired with `fire_trigger`; then it stops. **A fired task starts a cloud session wherever the scheduler puts it; this page promises no surface.** On 4 November 2026 two firings landed as Cowork-tagged cloud sessions attached to a claude.ai Project, left out of his code-session list, with the baton tools and no `create_session` ([#350](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/350) and its comments); an earlier one read itself as code mode (`library/upgrade-log.md`). The baton route stands on either (his go-ahead, 4 November 2026, [#325](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/325) item 1: *"I agree with all of your ideas. You are given permission to implement."*): its words carry all it needs. #350's `create_session` route failed its test on 5 November 2026 (a session it started was refused `create_session`, `add_repo` and `send_message`: [#350's comment of 13:40](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/350)) and is not a route. When nothing can move it starts none (*After a wait*). A task with a repeat or a run time is a clock and never wakes a session. **The clock lock** holds this by machine (agreed on [#371](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/371), 6 November 2026, filed as [#378](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/378), under his root on #363: *"Locks, not reminders."*): the size hook refuses a `create_trigger` or `update_trigger` carrying a `cron_expression` or `run_once_at`, and a `fire_trigger` unless the newest the session saw of that task shows it with neither: a `create_trigger` that went through, an `update_trigger` whose result shows the task's own entry with neither set (a field that entry leaves out is unset, as the platform's result of 6 November 2026 showed: `"cron_expression": ""` and no `run_once_at`), or a `list_triggers` entry showing both empty; an update's own words are never evidence, since one that omits the fields can leave an old schedule standing; first shown by a run on 6 November 2026, in the handover that fired this repository's successor from its own branch: it refused that fire on the platform's real result shape (the entry's omitted `run_once_at`), was fixed, and the fire then passed ([#363](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/363#issuecomment-6682020154)). **Its connectors** (his word, 9 November 2026, in the Hemz OS session, relayed on [Hemz-OS#285](https://github.com/Adonis80/Hemz-OS/issues/285#issuecomment-6744968242): *"yes, write it — load only the connectors a job needs."*): the task carries only the connectors its successor's job will call, since each one's tools load into every turn of that session, and one the job never calls is boot paid for nothing; one the job turns out to need is added at its next handover, named in that checkpoint as `Added:`. It then says "ready to start fresh session" naming the successor started. Starting none, or unable to, that line names *where* (a product's Project in Cowork; code mode, when needed, both repositories) and what to type. A successor that would wait on an approval is a permission, handled as `library/his-opener.md` says: parked, and taken by the next session he opens.

**The ticket** (his ruling, 5 November 2026, 11:07, filed on his word on [#363](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/363): *"Slim boot. A session loads one start page plus only what its job names; its boot size is measured and logged in the first checkpoint."*; the shape, a ticket and its check, is [#363's first comment](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/363#issuecomment-6656599619), written to his question of 11:15). A successor on this repository, the Juku OS Project's session's firing included, or on a product: `HOW-WE-BUILD.md` is its start page and the README stays its index. Its words carry a `Ticket:` line and under it, one item a line, this paragraph first (`how-we-build:library/session-changeover.md#The ticket`, which the check requires first), then the exact pieces its job needs beyond `HOW-WE-BUILD.md` and the README, each by address, its repository named first (`how-we-build:library/reviewer.md#…`, `Hemz-OS:PRODUCT.md#5`), a whole file only where the job needs all of it, and nothing in `juku-library/`, which is never loaded into a working session (`library/rulebook-files.md`). **A product's ticket** maps decision 0015's reads to addresses (`juku-library/DECISION-0015-JUKU-OS-AUDIT.md#Decision 0015 — the Juku OS audit: one owner per truth, fewer texts, read less (6 October 2026)`, item 6: *"`AGENTS.md` whole; the passages of `PRODUCT.md` and `roadmap.json` the task needs; no data moved"*): one product, named as the README's map spells it (`Hemz-OS:`), never a paused one; its `AGENTS.md` whole; `PRODUCT.md` by passage, never whole, its purpose and the rules it holds across every slice beside the job's own; `roadmap.json` by key, never whole, each of its notes it has (`_what_this_is`, `order_set`, `money_milestones`) and the item the job builds, its `retired` notes when an ideas review is due and a `done` item when its evidence is needed. Of the README's *Products under this rulebook* reads, the open items with their `next` lines are narrowed to the items its job names, on his ruling above (*"only what its job names"*); the queue past them is read at work, as `Added:`. Before firing, the outgoing session saves the words to a file and runs `python3 product-reads/ticket.py check --repo how-we-build=<its clone> <file>`, with `--repo <product>=<its clone>` for a product's: every address must resolve to one piece; this paragraph must be the first; the pieces total at most 40,000 tokens by its estimate (bytes ÷ 4); and a product's ticket must name one live product, this repository's `library/carries-sessions.md` whole (which each product session reads at its start, that page says), the product's `AGENTS.md` whole, at least one passage each of `PRODUCT.md` and `roadmap.json` and none but a note over 2,000 tokens and half its file, each of those three notes the roadmap has, an item, and nothing in the product's `-library/`. Red, it splits the job, and the successor is never handed the excess. Which passages serve the job, its purpose and cross-slice rules among them, is the outgoing session's choice, and nothing checks it: no reader sees a task's words, so a piece its successor finds missing goes in that successor's next checkpoint as `Added:` and the next ticket carries it. **After a wait** (#325 item 2, the same go-ahead). When nothing can move (a read, or a check it cannot watch, with no other ready item to build meanwhile: *Never idle on a read*, above), the session stays subscribed to the pull request's activity, writes its checkpoint, says so once on the pull request, and ends its turn: GitHub's event is the wake, never a clock; a surface that receives no such events, a Codex task among them, or one not yet shown to (so far only a code-mode cloud session has, `library/upgrade-log.md`), counts on none. Woken, it first reads the open pull requests of the repository it works in: a `Resumed:` or `Continued:` line newer than its checkpoint means another session holds the repository (one builder at a time), and it does nothing more; an event that unblocks nothing ends the turn again, saying nothing. Otherwise it carries on, first posting `Resumed: clean` on the item, which takes ownership back, since its checkpoint gave it up (`library/two-stacks.md`). A wait on him wakes nothing: his reply restarts the work.

**ARCHIVE** (#325 item 3, the same go-ahead). No tool archives or retitles a session, so a session's last message after its checkpoint, when no subscribed wait remains to wake it, ends with the word ARCHIVE, after its closing line: a label saying the session is finished, which he may archive from his list or leave. Nothing waits on it and no work rides on it, so it asks nothing of his hands.

**How much work** (his request, 4 November 2026, [#325](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/325) item 4: *"a clearly defined system that allows us to figure out how much work to give each session so that it approximately corresponds to the perfect time to hand over"*; agreed on 4 November 2026 by the CTO and the consultant's stand-in, the consultant being unreachable from a cloud session (`library/carries-sessions.md`), under `juku-library/DECISION-0023-STANDING-RULING.md#Decision 0023: an agreed, reviewed rule change counts as his ruling`; the debate is on [#326](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/pull/326)). Each item carries a class by its review rounds: S, one read; M, up to two rounds across pages; L, a gate, workflow or product slice with a journey. Every checkpoint records `Size: <session kind> boot <n>k end <n>k (<source>|unknown) items <#n class rounds, …>`, never a guess; with the size hook its source is `transcript`, and the hook refuses one off the real figure. At a boundary the next item is taken only if its class, by its kind's `Size:` records, is forecast to end under 300,000; with no records it is taken, and the line stops it as before. An item that does not fit hands over; none below it is taken first. The session that starts a successor writes `Planned: <kind> boot ~<n>k + <items by class> → end ~<n>k` (or `unknown`) in the task's words and its checkpoint. No page holds a weight: they live in those lines. The session that writes the fifth `Size:` line (found by searching the repository's comments for `Size:`) re-sets the classes from them in its successor's words or, if no session ended short with work ready, deletes this paragraph by pull request.
