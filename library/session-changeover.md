# Session changeover

Scope: every session, in either stack. Open when: deciding whether to stay in a session or start a fresh one, and before leaving one.

His rulings: 10 September 2026, weigh staying against starting fresh at every turn; 25 September, the session decides that itself; 26 September, no fresh session after barely any work. Decision 0006, revised as [decision 0009](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/114).

**One brief, and about 400,000 tokens** (his ruling, 9 October 2026, on [#174](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/174): *"hand over at about 400,000 tokens, or when a brief's goal is met."*). A session carries one brief, the work it was started with. Each session has one bounded deliverable and a stop condition. At its checkpoint, record available usage and cost evidence, then stop or hand over when the deliverable is met or blocked. No repeated investigation without new evidence (agreed 13 October 2026, [#204](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/204#issuecomment-6145865695), section 4). Reported plan usage is not cash and claims no saving until classified. At about 400,000 tokens of context it hands over whatever the progress; it reads its own count. A fresh session rebuilds its picture from the repository and the issues, never from the old chat; the handover note only points at them. This overrides amendment D of [decision 0016](../juku-library/DECISION-0016-LOCALISED-REVIEWS.md#D) (*"No new session rule"*), which that frozen record keeps as history. **Before a quiet hour, past about 150,000 tokens** (his ruling, 12 October 2026, on [#174](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/174): *"I agree with your second trigger. Make sure that this is applied globally."*). A session over about 150,000 tokens that is about to end a turn on a wait of an hour or more (on him, a review, or a check it cannot watch) hands over first, not after: resuming a large session after a long pause reloads its whole context, which costs more than a fresh session reading the handover; a wake after it only fires the successor (*After a wait*). Everything below works inside these three limits.

**The shape of the cost.** The boot paid is sunk. Compare remaining work, verification and likely rework under continuation and a fresh start. No cache-age, clock or load threshold decides it, and no token or idle threshold but the ones above. Boots routinely dominating planned product slices mean wrong slicing: fix it where `next` lines are written.

**A product session starts with both repositories attached** (agreed 13 October 2026, [#203](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/203), in #204 section 3): the product's and `Adonis80/how-we-build`. From the rulebook it may dispatch the existing asker (`ask-product-reads.yml`) and the recovery route (`review-product.yml`, `again` for a read that never finished: `library/product-joins.md`), and nothing else: a product session never changes rulebook files. The words that start one, and every handover to one, name both.

**Readiness before any deep read.** The first act on an item is to find, from its live body, the outcome asked and the next action this session can perform, verifying only the blockers deciding it: a secret, hands, his decision, an unmerged dependency. A `Ready:` line is a pointer, never an authority. A blocked action is parked with its unblock condition; the next eligible item is assessed. Missing information is unconfirmed readiness: settle it with a focused check; an investigation is its own bounded action.

**At a boundary** (slice merged, parked with its blocker written, or advisory question settled) the session decides, never by reflex. It continues to the next eligible item, same repository and stack, only when all hold: the item is within its brief and the session is short of about 400,000 tokens; continuing is preferable to starting fresh; that item's next action is ready; retained understanding demonstrably helps: the session can state the item's bounded outcome, the evidence it will reuse and the reading still needed without reconstructing the last item; the rulebook permits it (below); no degradation or hard-stop condition is present. Before acting it verifies the target's head and diff, checks, reviews and dependencies, and a working tree clear of the item left, then records on the item taken `Continued: <from> → <to> — main <sha> — reuse <what>; next <outcome>`. Otherwise it checkpoints and leaves.

**Rules that land meanwhile.** Fix the governing revision at boot and each accepted boundary transition; take on no later rules mid-slice; resuming mid-slice after a gap, verify the branch, pull request and `main` facts first. At a boundary compare rulebook `main` with that revision. Reassess required pages, read those added or changed, then adopt the verified revision. A change to `HOW-WE-BUILD.md`, a required rebootstrap or inability to apply the changed rules ends the session.

**Stop on degradation** inside any item, on any one of: the repository disproves a material state the session asserted; the repeated-failure signs `HOW-WE-BUILD.md` owns under *When something goes wrong*; the session cannot state the slice, next action and open findings without reconstructing them; the surface reports the history compacted. Then no new work: finish the operation in hand, write and verify the handover, leave. A routine check failing, or an expected repository change, is neither boundary nor degradation: diagnose it inside the item.

**Leaving.** Every durable fact goes into GitHub; the pull request and roadmap handover stand alone. A product session's closing handover answers *Any material system improvement found?* (`library/ideas-for-later.md`). The checkpoint comment names its cause: `Checkpoint: boundary — slice complete | parked: <blocker> | advisory settled`, or `Checkpoint: degradation — <sign>`; the next session's first comment: `Resumed: clean`, or `Resumed: corrected — <fact>, wrong at handover | changed since`. A fact wrong at handover counts against continuing only with evidence linking it to carried assumptions. These lines are evidence for revising this page, not a measure.

**A session starts its own successor, at once, never on a clock** (his ruling, 4 November 2026, 01:30, restated at 02:35: *"a session starts its own successor immediately, never on a clock; then do so at every handover"*; [#314](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/314), item 2). Only once it has decided it must end, and checked that the repository carries what the next session needs, nothing pasted, it starts the successor if, and only if, an item's next action is ready for it: the one standing task of the repository it works in (a product session: the product's) (created at first need with `create_trigger`, no repeat and no run time; later rewritten with `update_trigger`), its words naming the repositories to attach, the item, `Resumed:` first, fired with `fire_trigger`; then it stops. **A fired task starts a cloud session in code mode (claude.ai/code), never inside a claude.ai Project**: starting a session without him is a job Cowork cannot do (`library/two-stacks.md`) (his go-ahead, 4 November 2026, [#325](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/325) item 1: *"I agree with all of your ideas. You are given permission to implement."*): its words carry all it needs. A task with a repeat or a run time is a clock and never wakes a session. It then says "ready to start fresh session" naming the successor started. Starting none, or unable to, that line names *where* (a product's Project in Cowork; code mode, when needed, both repositories, the product's and `Adonis80/how-we-build`) and what to type. A successor that would wait on an approval is a permission, put to him once.

**After a wait** (#325 item 2, the same go-ahead). When nothing can move (a read, or a check it cannot watch), the session stays subscribed to the pull request's activity, writes its checkpoint, says so once on the pull request, and ends its turn: GitHub's event is the wake, never a clock; a surface that receives no such events, a Codex task among them, starts none and leaves the loop's one comment. Woken after a wait of an hour or more, a session past about 150,000 tokens (*Before a quiet hour*, above) only fires its successor; otherwise it carries on. Either way it first reads this repository's open pull requests: a `Resumed:` or `Continued:` line newer than its checkpoint means another session holds the repository (one builder at a time), and it does nothing more. A wait on him wakes nothing: his reply restarts the work.

**ARCHIVE** (#325 item 3, the same go-ahead). No tool archives or retitles a session, so a session's last message after its checkpoint, when no subscribed wait remains to wake it, ends with the word ARCHIVE, after its closing line, and he archives it by hand.

For Codex, the same continuation test applies across slices within a cloud task or thread.
