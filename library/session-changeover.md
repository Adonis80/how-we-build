# Session changeover

Scope: every session, in either stack. Open when: the Juku OS Project's session starts, deciding to stay or start fresh, and before leaving one.

His rulings: 10 September 2026, weigh staying against starting fresh at every turn; 25 September, the session decides that itself; 26 September, no fresh session after barely any work. Decision 0006, revised as [decision 0009](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/114).

**One brief, and 300,000 tokens, held by machine** (his rulings, 5 November 2026, 11:07, as filed on his word, "yes, file it", on [#363](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/363): *"Hard stop at 350,000 tokens, by machine. A hook reads the session's own transcript usage (the real figure, not an estimate) after every tool call: at 300,000 it requires the handover checkpoint; at 350,000 it blocks every action except writing that checkpoint. Every checkpoint carries a size line."*; this replaces his ruling of 9 October 2026 on [#174](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/174), *"hand over at about 400,000 tokens"*, whose brief clause stands: *"or when a brief's goal is met."*). A session carries one brief, the work it was started with: the queue it names, top first, until a limit or its goal is met (*How much work*, below; this widens #204's one deliverable to a queue, by the agreement cited there). At its checkpoint, record available usage and cost evidence, then stop or hand over when its brief is met or blocked. No repeated investigation without new evidence (agreed 13 October 2026, [#204](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/204#issuecomment-6145865695), section 4). Reported plan usage is not cash and claims no saving until classified. At 300,000 tokens of context it hands over whatever the progress. **The size hook** holds this: `.claude/settings.json` runs `.claude/size.py` before and after every tool call in a session started in a repository that carries it; it reads the newest main-thread turn's usage from the session's own transcript; past 300,000 it demands the checkpoint after every call until one has gone through (a refused or failed call is not one); past 350,000 it refuses every call but the checkpoint, loading a tool, and, once a checkpoint has gone through, starting the successor (*A session starts its own successor*); a checkpoint is a comment whose first line opens `Checkpoint:`, posted with the issue-comment tool, never the shell; at any size it refuses a checkpoint whose `Size:` line's `end` (or `now`) figure is not within 20k of the real one, and names the real one; `python3 .claude/size.py --now` prints it. An unreadable transcript blocks nothing and the hook says so. `check.sh` runs its selftest, which also holds the wiring. A session whose surface ran no hook (no `size hook: live` line at its first call) reads its own count and says so in its `Size:` line, source `unknown`. First shown on 5 November 2026, in the session that wrote it ([#366](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/pull/366)): once the files were on disk it ran on its calls and refused that session's own first checkpoint, whose `Size:` match a prose mention had caught (fixed, with its case). A session started on a surface that has not yet shown it says in its first checkpoint whether it ran. A fresh session rebuilds its picture from the repository and the issues, never from the old chat; the handover note only points at them. This overrides amendment D of [decision 0016](../juku-library/DECISION-0016-LOCALISED-REVIEWS.md#D) (*"No new session rule"*), which that frozen record keeps as history. **Before a quiet hour, past about 150,000 tokens** (his ruling, 12 October 2026, on [#174](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/174): *"I agree with your second trigger. Make sure that this is applied globally."*). A session over about 150,000 tokens that is about to end a turn on a wait of an hour or more (on him, a review, or a check it cannot watch) hands over first, not after: resuming a large session after a long pause reloads its whole context, which costs more than a fresh session reading the handover; woken after such a wait, it only fires the successor (*After a wait*). **Never idle on a read** (his word, 5 November 2026, on [#363](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/363#issuecomment-6665865830): *"if we load a session with lots of expensive context and then it sits idle for over an hour, the problem is that it's not doing any work in the meantime. So we want to avoid the situation where we have wasteful sessions like that … You are the CTO. Do your own research."*): a wait on a read or a check is not a quiet hour while another ready item can be built; the session builds it meanwhile (`HOW-WE-BUILD.md`, *The loop*: one waiting, one building, never more), and hands over before the wait only when nothing else can be built, or the second item is itself waiting. This narrows his ruling of 12 October 2026 above, on his word of 5 November 2026: idle time costs no tokens, but no work is done during it. Everything below works inside these three limits.

**The shape of the cost.** The boot paid is sunk. Compare remaining work, verification and likely rework under continuation and a fresh start. No cache-age, clock or load threshold decides it, and no token or idle threshold but the ones above. Boots routinely dominating planned product slices mean wrong slicing: fix it where `next` lines are written.

**A product session starts with both repositories attached** (agreed 13 October 2026, [#203](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/203), in #204 section 3): the product's and `Adonis80/how-we-build`. From the rulebook it may dispatch the existing asker (`ask-product-reads.yml`) and the recovery route (`review-product.yml`, `again` for a read that never finished: `library/product-joins.md`), and nothing else: a product session never changes rulebook files. The words that start one, and every handover to one, name both.

**Readiness before any deep read.** The first act on an item is to find, from its live body, the outcome asked and the next action this session can perform, verifying only the blockers deciding it: a secret, hands, his decision, an unmerged dependency. A `Ready:` line is a pointer, never an authority. A blocked action is parked with its unblock condition; the next eligible item is assessed. Missing information is unconfirmed readiness: settle it with a focused check; an investigation is its own bounded action.

**At a boundary** (slice merged, parked with its blocker written, or advisory question settled) the session decides, never by reflex. It continues to the next eligible item, same repository and stack, only when all hold: the item is within its brief and the session is forecast to end it under 300,000 tokens (*How much work*); continuing is preferable to starting fresh; that item's next action is ready; retained understanding demonstrably helps: the session can state the item's bounded outcome, the evidence it will reuse and the reading still needed without reconstructing the last item; the rulebook permits it (below); no degradation or hard-stop condition is present. Before acting it verifies the target's head and diff, checks, reviews and dependencies, and a working tree clear of the item left, then records on the item taken `Continued: <from> → <to> — main <sha> — reuse <what>; next <outcome>`. Otherwise it checkpoints and leaves.

**Rules that land meanwhile.** Fix the governing revision at boot and each accepted boundary transition; take on no later rules mid-slice; resuming mid-slice after a gap, verify the branch, pull request and `main` facts first. At a boundary compare rulebook `main` with that revision. Reassess required pages, read those added or changed, then adopt the verified revision. A change to `HOW-WE-BUILD.md`, a required rebootstrap or inability to apply the changed rules ends the session.

**Stop on degradation** inside any item, on any one of: the repository disproves a material state the session asserted; the repeated-failure signs `HOW-WE-BUILD.md` owns under *When something goes wrong*; the session cannot state the slice, next action and open findings without reconstructing them; the surface reports the history compacted. Then no new work: finish the operation in hand, write and verify the handover, leave. A routine check failing, or an expected repository change, is neither boundary nor degradation: diagnose it inside the item.

**Leaving.** Every durable fact goes into GitHub; the pull request and roadmap handover stand alone. A product session's closing handover answers *Any material system improvement or independently sellable piece found?* (`library/ideas-for-later.md`). The checkpoint comment names its cause: `Checkpoint: boundary — slice complete | parked: <blocker> | advisory settled`, or `Checkpoint: degradation — <sign>`, or head office's release (*The Juku OS Project's session*), which carries no `Size:` line and counts as no record; the next session's first comment: `Resumed: clean`, or `Resumed: corrected — <fact>, wrong at handover | changed since`. Each `Checkpoint:`, `Resumed:` and `Continued:` line ends with its session's link where its surface gives one (a code-mode session's own address), so a reader can match it to the run that started it. A fact wrong at handover counts against continuing only with evidence linking it to carried assumptions. These lines are evidence for revising this page, not a measure.

**A session starts its own successor, at once, never on a clock** (his ruling, 4 November 2026, 01:30, restated at 02:35: *"a session starts its own successor immediately, never on a clock; then do so at every handover"*; [#314](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/314), item 2). Only once it has decided it must end, and checked that the repository carries what the next session needs, nothing pasted, it starts the successor if, and only if, an item's next action is ready for it (a parked permission act is ready only on its unblock condition: `library/his-opener.md`): the one standing task of the repository it works in (a product session: the product's; created at first need with `create_trigger`, no repeat and no run time; later rewritten with `update_trigger`), its words naming the repositories to attach, `HOW-WE-BUILD.md` read whole first, the checkpoint it starts from, its ticket where *The ticket* (below) gives it one, the queue, its `Planned:` line, and `Resumed:` first unless a `Resumed:` or `Continued:` line is newer than that checkpoint, which means another session holds the repository and it does nothing more (*After a wait*), fired with `fire_trigger`; then it stops. **A fired task starts a cloud session wherever the scheduler puts it; this page promises no surface.** On 4 November 2026 two firings landed as Cowork-tagged cloud sessions attached to a claude.ai Project, left out of his code-session list, with the baton tools and no `create_session` ([#350](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/350) and its comments); an earlier one read itself as code mode (`library/upgrade-log.md`). The baton route stands on either (his go-ahead, 4 November 2026, [#325](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/325) item 1: *"I agree with all of your ideas. You are given permission to implement."*): its words carry all it needs. #350's `create_session` route failed its test on 5 November 2026 (a session it started was refused `create_session`, `add_repo` and `send_message`: [#350's comment of 13:40](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/350)) and is not a route. When nothing can move it starts none (*After a wait*). A task with a repeat or a run time is a clock and never wakes a session. It then says "ready to start fresh session" naming the successor started. Starting none, or unable to, that line names *where* (a product's Project in Cowork; code mode, when needed, both repositories) and what to type. A successor that would wait on an approval is a permission, put to him once.

**The ticket** (his ruling, 5 November 2026, 11:07, filed on his word on [#363](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/363): *"Slim boot. A session loads one start page plus only what its job names; its boot size is measured and logged in the first checkpoint."*; the shape, a ticket and its check, is [#363's first comment](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/363#issuecomment-6656599619), written to his question of 11:15). For now a successor on this repository alone, the Juku OS Project's session's firing included; `HOW-WE-BUILD.md` is its start page and the README stays its index; a product session reads as the README's *Products under this rulebook* says (decision 0015) until products' tickets land, the next slice of #363's boot work, which maps those reads to addresses. Its words carry a `Ticket:` line and under it, one item a line, this paragraph first (`how-we-build:library/session-changeover.md#The ticket`, which the check requires first), then the exact pieces of this repository its job needs beyond `HOW-WE-BUILD.md` and the README, each by address (`how-we-build:library/reviewer.md#…`), a whole file only where the job needs all of it, and nothing in `juku-library/`, which is never loaded into a working session (`library/rulebook-files.md`). Those words are the ticket's one home: each handover's `update_trigger` replaces them whole, never appending. Before firing, the outgoing session saves the words to a file and runs `python3 product-reads/ticket.py check --repo how-we-build=<its clone> <file>`: every address must resolve to one piece of this repository, this paragraph must be the first, and the pieces total at most 40,000 tokens by its estimate (bytes ÷ 4); red, it splits the job, and the successor is never handed the excess. **The guard** holds this by machine, not by memory (his root on #363, 5 November 2026: *"every rule above relied on a session remembering to obey prose. Locks, not reminders."*): the size hook (*One brief*, above), in a repository carrying `product-reads/ticket.py` (this one; a product's copy of the hook does nothing of this), refuses a `create_trigger` or `update_trigger` whose words (the tools' `prompt`, as their schemas read on 5 November 2026) carry a ticket failing that check, naming its faults, and a `fire_trigger` until such words have gone through in the same session; not yet shown by a run, the handover after it lands is its first. Its boot reads `HOW-WE-BUILD.md` whole, then the README, the checkpoint its words name, the open pull requests and the item's live body (*Readiness before any deep read*), and each piece with `python3 product-reads/ticket.py --repo how-we-build=<its clone> read <address>`: no library page but its ticket's pieces, no older checkpoint and no whole issue thread at boot; a surface without Python reads the same pieces by hand. The guard holds the first of those: from the first call of a session whose task words (its first message before any reply that carries a `Ticket:` line) name a ticket until its `Resumed:`, `Continued:` or `Checkpoint:` comment has gone through, it refuses a `Read` or `Grep` of a file in this repository other than `HOW-WE-BUILD.md`, the README or a whole-file piece, and a shell command naming a rule page (`library/`, `juku-library/`, `CHARTER.md`, `AGENTS.md`, `RICH-DATA.md`) that is neither a whole-file piece nor, in a `ticket.py` command, a piece's file; it does not see a recursive search or a read dressed as a ticket read, and the older checkpoints and threads stay the session's to leave. Once at work, a read a live rule calls for (a page whose index row fires, a boundary's `Size:` search under *How much work*) is taken as before, and goes in its next checkpoint as `Added: <address> — <why>`, so the next ticket carries what the job turned out to need. **The boot measured**, the ruling's second half, with his weigh-in of 5 November 2026, 18:15, on [#363](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/363#issuecomment-6665666753) (*"I agree to your upgrade"*: *"every fresh session, before any work, posts one line on its issue: its starting size, split into (a) what the platform loads that we can't touch … and (b) what we made it read … Target for (b): under 30k. Over target → that same line names the three heaviest pieces it read"*): in a repository carrying `product-reads/ticket.py`, the size hook refuses a session's first `Resumed:` or `Continued:` comment unless it carries a `Boot:` line with the hook's own figures, `ours` being every tool result read before it (bytes ÷ 4, a loaded tool's schema excepted) and `platform` the rest of its real size, and past 30k ours its three heaviest reads; the refusal hands the line to paste, since a hook cannot post a comment itself. The rest of that weigh-in is not yet held by machine: the trim of those pieces goes in that session's next checkpoint as its next ticket's cut, and three boots over target in a row are flagged by hand on the queue's issue. Replayed on the firing of 5 November 2026, 18:35, up to its `Resumed:`: `platform 122k, ours 20k`. First carried by the firing of 5 November 2026 that built the guard ([#372](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/pull/372)): its six pieces served, nothing was added at boot, and its size at the first call was 97k (transcript); the guard, replayed on that session's own transcript up to its `Resumed:` line, refused a library page off its ticket and passed every read the boot had made.

**After a wait** (#325 item 2, the same go-ahead). When nothing can move (a read, or a check it cannot watch, with no other ready item to build meanwhile: *Before a quiet hour*, above), the session stays subscribed to the pull request's activity, writes its checkpoint, says so once on the pull request, and ends its turn: GitHub's event is the wake, never a clock; a surface that receives no such events, a Codex task among them, or one not yet shown to (so far only a code-mode cloud session has, `library/upgrade-log.md`), counts on none. Woken, it first reads the open pull requests of the repository it works in: a `Resumed:` or `Continued:` line newer than its checkpoint means another session holds the repository (one builder at a time), and it does nothing more; an event that unblocks nothing ends the turn again, saying nothing. Otherwise, after a wait of an hour or more, a session past about 150,000 tokens (*Before a quiet hour*, above) spends one short turn firing its successor; any other carries on, first posting `Resumed: clean` on the item, which takes ownership back, since its checkpoint gave it up (`library/two-stacks.md`). A wait on him wakes nothing: his reply restarts the work.

**The Juku OS Project's session** (head office; his question of 4 November 2026 is recorded only in the CTO's words, on [#325](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/325#issuecomment-6629980663) item 5, and sharpened there at 12:30 after a firing while a run was alive made two builders, so this rests on the agreement alone: agreed on 4 November 2026 by the CTO and the consultant's stand-in, the consultant being unreachable from a cloud session (`library/carries-sessions.md`), under `juku-library/DECISION-0023-STANDING-RULING.md#Decision 0023: an agreed, reviewed rule change counts as his ruling`; the debate is on [#338](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/pull/338)). He opens it himself and it sees none of the fired sessions' chats, so before anything else it reads the rulebook's chain: the standing task's last run (`list_triggers`: status, when fired, which session; not yet shown read from a Project session, `library/upgrade-log.md`), the open pull requests, and the newest `Checkpoint:`, `Resumed:` and `Continued:` lines. The chain holds the repository while that run is pending or running, or was fired after the newest checkpoint and has not failed; while a line is newer than that checkpoint (*After a wait*), unless the task's last run started the line's session (its link names it) and has failed; or while that checkpoint waits on a read or check not yet finished. Then it builds and fires nothing here (one builder at a time). Otherwise the chain has dropped: an act parked for his consent (`library/his-opener.md`) it takes itself, once his opener in its own session covers it, and fires nothing for it; with any other item ready it first releases a failed run's line by posting `Checkpoint: released — <the line's link>, its run failed before any checkpoint`, which makes the line older than a checkpoint for a successor's starter check (*A session starts its own successor*), then rewrites the task's words for that work, naming the newest checkpoint as a successor's starter does (*A session starts its own successor*), then fires it, never offering to (*Who decides*), since the chain builds without him; with none it fires nothing. The release is agreed on 4 November 2026 by the CTO and the consultant's stand-in, the consultant unreachable from a cloud session, under decision 0023, the debate on [#347](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/pull/347), re-cut to a failed run alone after its second read; none has yet run. Its first reply tells him in one line what is running and what landed. Without the task tools, or reading no last run, it fires nothing, judges from the pull requests alone, and says so in that line.

**ARCHIVE** (#325 item 3, the same go-ahead). No tool archives or retitles a session, so a session's last message after its checkpoint, when no subscribed wait remains to wake it, ends with the word ARCHIVE, after its closing line: a label saying the session is finished, which he may archive from his list or leave. Nothing waits on it and no work rides on it, so it asks nothing of his hands.

**How much work** (his request, 4 November 2026, [#325](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/325) item 4: *"a clearly defined system that allows us to figure out how much work to give each session so that it approximately corresponds to the perfect time to hand over"*; agreed on 4 November 2026 by the CTO and the consultant's stand-in, the consultant being unreachable from a cloud session (`library/carries-sessions.md`), under `juku-library/DECISION-0023-STANDING-RULING.md#Decision 0023: an agreed, reviewed rule change counts as his ruling`; the debate is on [#326](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/pull/326)). Each item carries a class by its review rounds: S, one read; M, up to two rounds across pages; L, a gate, workflow or product slice with a journey. Every checkpoint records `Size: <session kind> boot <n>k end <n>k (<source>|unknown) items <#n class rounds, …>`, never a guess; with the size hook its source is `transcript`, and the hook refuses one off the real figure. At a boundary the next item is taken only if its class, by its kind's `Size:` records, is forecast to end under 300,000; with no records it is taken, and the line stops it as before. An item that does not fit hands over; none below it is taken first. The session that starts a successor writes `Planned: <kind> boot ~<n>k + <items by class> → end ~<n>k` (or `unknown`) in the task's words and its checkpoint. No page holds a weight: they live in those lines. The session that writes the fifth `Size:` line (found by searching the repository's comments for `Size:`) re-sets the classes from them in its successor's words or, if no session ended short with work ready, deletes this paragraph by pull request.

For Codex, the same continuation test applies across slices within a cloud task or thread.
