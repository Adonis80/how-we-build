# HyperSolid: the Juku style

Scope: every product's screens, either stack. Open when: a screen is designed, built or reviewed, or a design brief is written.

## His beauty standards: six, each counted

He has few, and a builder holds its screen to all six before anyone else sees it (his words, 27 November 2026, filed on [#433](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/433#issuecomment-7229770889): *"we have beauty standards, which were derived from the approved artifact. Those beauty standards should be quite easy to follow. There's not many of them. For example, how much information is displayed on one screen at any one time. … Text should be of a certain size and a certain color, and spacing is also specified. … And we should not need a cold reader to correct the builder. The builder should know what it is supposed to do in the first place."*). The artifact is the formula screen he approved by picture on 4 November 2026 (https://claude.ai/artifact/85YmnKiJdRTG3saj96mJKh), and it sets the standard in every product (the same filing: *"I made explicitly clear that the artifact was to dictate the beauty standard across all Juku products"*). The words quoted in the table are his of 28 November 2026, 01:29 UTC (filed whole on [#433](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/433#issuecomment-7231801382)), save *"like a tree"*, which is in the first filing. Every count was taken from the artifact on 28 November 2026: its nine levels drawn at 390 by 844 and counted by machine, and its own sheet read (`2026-10-30-R6-style.css`), whose values `library/juku-style.css` carries one for one, checked again that day. They are what the artifact shows, which is what his approval covers (decision 0024); how much a screen shows is among the standards he says are derived from it, above.

A standard is met by a count, never by a yes: count the screen, give the six counts with the self-check ((e), below), and where a count is over take the remedy on its line. The remedy is never a smaller size or a tighter gap, and never a question to him (`library/hypersolid.md`, *His look guide*). A word here is anything with a letter in it; a bare figure or a sign is not one. The artifact's levels are rows of names and figures; a page of reading (a transcript, an article) is no part the kit draws yet (*What the kit does not draw yet*, below), and standard 1's 39 is not its count.

## (a) The feel

Words and figures sit straight on a near-black ground, like a private-bank statement: no cards and no boxes, and no line in the content but one faint hairline above each total and the lines a control draws (a choice's edge, the typed line, the tabs' line). Colour shows up only where it means something: gold for money and for the actions you take, the edge of what you chose or are typing in included, turquoise for what was measured, green for done, red for a stop. Everything else is warm off-white in three shades. Tapping a part makes it the whole screen. The level you came from drops back, blurred and dimmed, so you always know where you are, and the working sits low on the screen within thumb reach.

## (c) Always / Never

**Always**
- Put content straight on the ground.
- Draw a hairline above each total and nowhere else in the content. The top bar's rule is the only other line, save the lines that are part of a control: a choice's border, the typed line's underline (`.j-field`), the tabs' hairline with its gold line (`.j-tabs`) and an option's edge (`.j-option`).
- On a level that adds something up, lay rows out as one equation, left to right: operator, name, figure, ›. Keep the operator column there even when it is empty, so the names line up.
- Push the equation to the bottom of the level (`.j-foot`), so the screen's whole height is used. The title gets the space above it, and the thumb reaches the rows.
- Keep sections clearly apart, each under its band heading (`.j-band-row`), never by a line or a box.
- Colour every figure by what it means: gold for money, turquoise for anything measured (its unit too), ink for plain percentages.
- Use tabular figures. Put a unit straight after its figure, smaller (12.5px), in the same colour. A space goes before %, as in "+25 %".
- Write crumbs, bands, the brand and total labels in widely spaced capitals. Only the level title is in sentence case.
- Make every tap target at least 44px tall: rows 68px, steps 52px, picks 52px.
- Use the action colour as text for back, add and a selected choice (`.j-btn.is-on`; a chosen tab is ink over the gold line, `.j-tabs`) (on Hemz, gold, which its approved screen shares with money; another product, the colour its constitution gives actions). Back names the level it goes back to.
- Make a tapped part the whole view, with at most two levels showing behind it, blurred. With reduced motion, the screen just changes, with no movement.
- Put the › only on rows that open.
- Give each level its title, a line saying what it adds up to, and its parts, as the map gives them. How much a level shows, its spacing, when it scrolls and when a part opens a layer of its own are standards 1 to 4 (*His beauty standards*, above).

**Never**
- Never put content in boxes, cards, panels, borders or row dividers. The only filled thing is the (i) sheet; controls carry none (his picks of 12 November 2026, above).
- Never use a flat gold button or a gold background behind words. Gold is for text and edges; nothing carries a gold fill, a selected choice included (his word of 12 November 2026, above; the screens it was lifted from showed one, and his later word supersedes them there).
- Never use a colour without its meaning. Turquoise is never money, and gold is never a measurement.
- Never put the serif on anything except the hero money.
- Never put explanations on the screen. They go behind the (i).
- Never add shadows, glows or gradients to content. The only ones are the bar fade, the deep-level veil and the sheet's shadow.
- Never round corners past 2px on controls.
- Never introduce a size, colour, gap or timing that is not in `library/juku-style.css`, save the product's own colours from its constitution and a new part he approved by picture, added here (*What the kit does not draw yet*).

**The style lock** holds the first of these by machine (his ruling, 5 November 2026, 11:07, on [#363](https://github.com/Adonis80/how-we-build/issues/363): *"Style lock. A machine check on every build flags boxes, cards or borders round content (a highlighted control excepted, one hairline above a total); the reviewer compares the rest against the agreed reference build."*). `product-reads/style-lock.py build <paths>`, run by a product's `verify` on every build over its screens' own stylesheets, markup and scripts (`library/carries-sessions.md`, *Its machinery*), fails a border, outline or shadow on anything but a control or a top hairline on a total, a fill on anything but the page's ground and the (i) sheet (a control's fill included, save a press's flash and a line it draws, since controls carry none: his picks of 12 November 2026, above), a utility class drawing the same, and an element dressed as a control it is not (a choice class or `.j-field` on something that is no control, or a choice class on a typed field, which is drawn as `.j-field`), naming the file and line. A script (`.js`, `.mjs`, `.cjs`, `.ts`) is read for the markup it writes: the tags in its strings, judged as markup, and the classes and styles it puts on an element it makes with `createElement`, judged as that tag; on an element it did not make only a fill is flagged, and a class name a script computes is the reviewer's (the machine exam of 13 November 2026 in Hemz OS: the lock read no script, so a screen drawn by one passed unread). The kit's own stylesheet, read from here, is not the product's to scan. Everything else on this page, and anything the lock cannot see, the product's reviewer compares against the agreed reference build: the product's saved design (`library/hypersolid.md`, steps 6 and 7), and for the kit itself the formula screen above. `check.sh` runs its cases and `style-lock.py kit`: the stylesheet loads nothing from outside (no `@import`, no `url()` but `data:`), since every product loads it live, every value table (b) names is in it (#365's advisories), and no rule of it sets text size or letter spacing on a bare element from under a class, where it would outrank the kit's own control classes (the machine exam of 13 November 2026 in Hemz OS: the page's rule for every `button` and `input` drew Add at 13.5px where table (b) says 11.5px, the typed line and tabs at 13.5px where it says 16px, and no control's .02em; that rule now sits inside `:where()`, which weighs nothing, and no value changed). Not yet shown by a run in a product: no product's `verify` carries it yet, and the first that does is its first.

## (d) How a new screen is made from this

1. **Load the kit.** Load `library/juku-style.css` read from this repository at a pinned rulebook commit by the product's build, never a copy kept or edited in the product (`library/carries-sessions.md`, *Its pages*: "read from this repository, never copied down"); the product's own stylesheet sets every one of the kit's colour tokens from its constitution, its own approved themes included, so no product shows Hemz's colours by default. No product build does this yet: the first to do it shows it working. Put `.j-page` on the body and `.j-screen` on the root, and leave `data-theme` unset: light and midnight wait for his picture (above).
2. **Build the frame.** `.j-stage` holds one `.j-level` per level, with `.is-deep` on every level below the top one. `.j-bar` sits after the stage and contains back or brand, then `.j-bar-rule`, the (i) and Done. `.j-info` is optional and opens from the (i). A build the product runs puts `.is-fill` on the root `.j-screen`, so it fills a phone's window and stands centred at 390px on anything wider; a board or picture leaves it off and draws at 390 × 844. A wider window's own use of its space is the desktop composition (*What the kit does not draw yet*), never this.
3. **Map the content from the product's map.** Each level gets `.j-head` (crumb, title, lead) and then a `.j-eqn.j-foot` of `.j-row`s in the map's order. Each row's operator comes from the maths, and each figure is coloured by its meaning. Every subtotal and total is a `.j-sum`.
4. **Use only `.j-` classes.** Write no inline styles (save step 5's `--i`, `--j-tab-x` and `--j-tab-w`) and no new values. If something has no class here, it is a design question: it goes to the designer by HyperSolid's steps 3 to 6, and don't invent a fix (*What the kit does not draw yet*).
5. **Wire the drill-in.** Tapping a row sets the levels to `.is-on`, `.is-behind-1` or `.is-behind-2` (`.is-off` for anything else) and puts `.is-forward` on the screen. Back drops the class from the screen and sets the leaving level to `.is-off`. Mark each arriving row `.j-in` with `--i`. Make the levels behind `inert`. On a row of choices, set `--j-tab-x` and `--j-tab-w` on `.j-tabs` to the chosen tab's left offset and width, so the gold line slides under it. A small set of options, each with a figure and its effect, sits under its band as a rail: `<div class="j-rail" role="radiogroup" aria-labelledby="<the band's id>">` holding one `<label class="j-option"><input type="radio" name="<a name no other rail on the screen shares>" value="…">` with `.j-option-name`, `.j-option-fig` and `.j-option-line` per option, one `checked`; it sits in a level, whose gutters it bleeds through. On `change`, scroll the chosen option into view (`scrollIntoView({inline:"nearest",block:"nearest"})`). Several rails stack down the level, one per band.

## (e) Self-check: the six counts, then yes to all ten

First give the six counts of *His beauty standards* (above) for the screen, each beside its limit. Then:
